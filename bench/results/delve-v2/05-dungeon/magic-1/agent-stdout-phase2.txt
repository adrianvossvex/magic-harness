## Core

`game.undo()` pops a snapshot from `state.history`, copies it back **into the same state object** (so anything holding `game.state` keeps working), and restores the generator from the snapshot's `rngState`. It returns `true` when a turn was undone and `false` otherwise.

- Every turn-costing action takes a deep snapshot of the state *before* it runs (excluding `history`/`undo`, so nothing nests); free actions — wall bumps, empty pickups, an invalid item index — throw their snapshot away and leave the window untouched. `state.history` holds up to ten, oldest first, and `state.undo` mirrors its length.
- Because the snapshot carries tiles, monsters, items, log, depth, `turn`, `nextId` and `rngState`, an undo is exact: a rewind right after `>` puts the previous level, its monsters and its loot back.
- `hash()` now serialises the state minus `history` and `undo`, so it describes the game, not the rewind window — hash before an action equals hash after undoing it, and replaying after a rewind reproduces the same monsters, messages and hash (verified four rounds deep on a generated level).
- `applyKey(game, "z")` returns `["You rewind time."]` or `["Nothing to rewind."]` without touching the log, which is what keeps that hash equality honest. `runScript` picks it up for free.

## Page

`z` rewinds (it also works on the death screen, so a fatal turn can be taken back), the HUD reads `Undo N` next to hp/depth/turn/level/xp/gold, and the log and pack refresh from the rewound state. `renderer.rewind()` makes the next frame snap meshes and camera into place instead of sliding, adds a particle ripple at the player plus a short shake, and a "You rewind time." toast confirms it; a rewind that removes a whole level does not fire kill bursts. The help and title screens list the key.
